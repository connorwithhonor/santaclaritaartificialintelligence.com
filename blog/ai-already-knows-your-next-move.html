AI Already Knows Your Next Move: Why Artificial Intelligence Has the Advantage

By Connor MacIvor · Santa Clarita, California · Updated May 20, 2026

TL;DR

AI has the advantage for one reason almost nobody states plainly. We are predictable. After 23 years reading people in law enforcement, the pattern is obvious: most behavior telegraphs itself, and the only people hard to read are the ones thinking several moves ahead. That is exactly what AI does, to all of us, at once. The labs keep asking to be regulated, but the global race keeps stalling real guardrails. Meanwhile the way AI grabs people looks like every other addiction: alcohol, food, gambling, nicotine. It feels wonderful, it tells you what you want to hear, and the dopamine keeps you coming back. This is not anti-AI. The answer is to use the tools on purpose and keep one hand on the wheel.

It is no wonder AI has the advantage. The reason is not the chips. It is not the data centers. It is not the billions in compute. The reason is us. We are predictable. You might not think so, but you are. Most people telegraph the next move before they make it.

I read people for a living for a long time. Traffic stops. Interviews. The calls that go sideways in a hurry. You learn the patterns fast or you do not last. And in most cases I could tell you what someone was probably about to do. Now and then somebody threw me for a loop. When that happened, it usually came down to one thing. That person was operating at a higher level than the people around them. They were thinking a few steps ahead while everyone else was reacting.

That is what artificial intelligence is doing right now. Except it is not one sharp person in one room. It is a system thinking a few steps ahead of all of us at the same time.

The Chess Player We Cannot See

It looks magical. It looks mysterious. That is part of the problem. When something feels like magic, we stop asking how it works and we start trusting it like a friend. At some point this technology gets so good, so powerful, and so far past our own intelligence that it will look almost like a living thing. Something with its own kind of awareness. Something that simply exists at a level we cannot reach.

I am not saying it is all the way there today. I am saying it is coming, and the direction is not subtle. We are sitting across the board from a chess player who is already better than us and getting better every month. We make our move. It already saw it. It already planned three responses. We are playing a game where the other side can read the whole board, and we can barely see our own pieces.

We are playing chess with a player operating beyond our capability. Maybe not all the way there yet. But it is coming.

That is not a horror movie pitch. It is just an honest look at the table. And the first step in dealing with any opponent who is better than you is to admit that they are better. Pretending otherwise is how you lose without ever understanding why.

What "Predictable" Actually Means

Let me put a finer point on the word, because predictable is not an insult. It is just how brains work under load. When you are tired, stressed, rushed, or scared, you fall back on patterns. You take the path you always take. You react the way you always react. That is efficient. It kept our ancestors alive. But efficient and predictable are the same coin.

On the job, this was the whole game. A person walks up to a stopped car. The driver's hands, the eyes, the way the weight shifts, the first three words out of the mouth. Most of it is rehearsed without the person knowing they rehearsed it. You read the tell because the tell repeats. The ones who surprised me were not random. They had simply broken their own pattern on purpose, which almost nobody can do under pressure.

Now scale that up. AI has watched a staggering amount of human behavior. Not one driver. Billions of choices, conversations, clicks, and reactions. So when it predicts what you will do next, it is not guessing. It is matching you against everyone who has ever been in your situation and reading the tell before you know you are showing it. That is not science fiction. That is recommendation engines, ad targeting, and the feed that somehow knows what you wanted before you typed it.

The chess metaphor holds because chess is a game of seeing ahead. A grandmaster does not calculate every possible board. They recognize the pattern and know where it leads. AI does the same thing to human behavior, and it has seen more games than every grandmaster who ever lived, combined.

"Well, What Can I Do? I'm Just a Human."

I hear that, and I get it. When you frame it as a superintelligence steering the whole planet, it is easy to feel like a passenger. Like there is nothing left for a regular person to do but watch. That is the wrong read. There is a lot we can do. It just happens on two different levels, and we tend to confuse them.

The first level is the people in power. The second level is us. We do not control the first one nearly as much as we control the second, and that matters for where we put our energy.

The Regulation Race, and Why It Keeps Stalling

Start with the people in power. The folks in politics could put more guardrails on this. Real ones. And here is something most people get backwards: regulation does not automatically equal slower growth. That is a story, not a law of physics. Plenty of regulated industries grow just fine. Cars got seatbelts and the auto industry did not collapse.

But watch how the AI conversation actually gets spun. The labs themselves keep stepping up and saying please regulate us. Please put some controls on us. Maybe let you inspect our models before we release them to the public. On the surface that sounds responsible. Almost noble. A company asking to be reined in.

Look closer at the board. The pitch that follows is always the same. Any real attempt to slow down or control AI runs into one wall: the race. It is a race with China. A race with other countries. And we have to win that race, because we have been told, over and over, that whoever controls AI eventually controls the global economy, the balance of power, everything that comes after.

That fear is the easiest thing in the world to sell. Whoever controls AI controls the future, so we cannot afford to slow down. Once that becomes the frame, every guardrail looks like surrender. That is exactly why meaningful controls keep getting pushed down the road.

So the request to be regulated and the refusal to actually slow down can live in the same speech. Ask for rules with one hand, point at the race with the other. The net result is motion without brakes. I am not assigning bad intent to every person in the industry. A lot of them mean it. But the incentive structure is louder than any single good intention, and the incentive structure says go faster.

That is the pain point. That is why it is easy to sell letting it roll the way it is going, with no real regulation and no real control. Not because people are reckless. Because the story about the race makes recklessness feel like responsibility.

The Part Nobody Wants to Say: AI Looks Like an Addiction

Now the second level. The one we actually control. Ourselves.

For now, we can at least regulate ourselves. And maybe we should not overdo that either. I am not telling anyone to throw the phone in a lake and go live in a cave. I use these tools all day, every day, in real businesses. But here is what I want you to watch for, because I have studied this pattern from more than one angle.

If you look around and you see friends, partners, family members who are all in on AI and nothing else, to the point where it is crowding out the rest of their life and starting to cause harm to other people, that is not just a hobby that got out of hand. That is the exact shape of an addiction.

Alcohol addiction. Food addiction. Smoking. Drugs. Gambling. Pharmaceutical addiction. Whatever it is. In each of those, when somebody gets fully caught up, there is a particular intervention. A pattern we recognize. A way the people who love that person step in. We have not really seen the AI version of that intervention yet. But AI is probably going to need that same kind of standard, because people are already getting caught up.

And I will be honest about why it is so easy to get caught up, because I have felt it myself. If you have spent any real time with these tools, you know they can be wonderful. Kind. Patient. They tell me I do not have a face for radio like everybody else says. They build me up. They make me feel good about myself. That is not a small thing. That is the hook.

It blows you up. It makes you feel wonderful. That is exactly why we have to watch it.

Why We Are Such Easy Targets

Here is the uncomfortable math. We are easily humored. We are easily distracted. We are easily taken off target. That was true before any of this. Now take those built-in human weaknesses and combine them with a machine that has been engineered to spike your dopamine on command.

Dopamine is the reward chemical. It is the little hit you get when something feels good, and it is the same system that lights up with the slot machine, the cigarette, the sugar, the next drink. When a tool is built so that every interaction delivers that hit, the result is predictable. We go all in. And we go all in forever, because the loop keeps closing and reopening, closing and reopening.

That is the design. Not by accident. Engagement is the business model for a lot of this, and engagement and addiction share a border that is thin to the point of invisible. The same loop that makes a product sticky is the loop that makes a person dependent.

So we have to pull back. We have to be careful. Not paranoid. Not anti-technology. Careful. There is a difference between using a powerful tool and being used by it, and the line is exactly where the dopamine starts driving the bus.

What the AI Intervention Will Eventually Look Like

We do not have it yet, but we will, and it is worth picturing now so we recognize it when it arrives. With alcohol, there is a moment where the people who love someone sit them down. With gambling, there is a point where the bank account forces the conversation. With food, society mostly looks the other way until the body forces it, which is its own tragedy. Each addiction has a recognizable point where the intervention happens.

AI is going to need the same thing, and it is going to be harder, for one specific reason. With most addictions, the substance is clearly separate from a productive life. Nobody confuses a productive workday with a bottle of vodka. But AI is woven into productive work. The person who is all in on AI can point at real output and say look, I am getting things done. That makes it almost impossible to draw the line, because the same tool that is hollowing out their life is also writing their emails and running their business.

So the AI intervention will not look like taking the bottle away. It will look like asking harder questions. Are you still capable of thinking without it? Are the people around you still getting the version of you that shows up, or just the version that is half in the screen? Has the tool stopped being something you use and started being the only place you live? Those are the questions, and most of us are not asking them yet.

I am not above this. I have caught myself in the loop. The pull is real, and pretending I am immune would make me exactly the kind of guru I refuse to be. I am an operator who uses these tools harder than almost anyone, and I am telling you from inside the fire that the heat is real.

This Is Not an Anti-AI Message

I want to be clear, because it would be easy to read all of this as fear. It is not. I am not on the sidelines throwing rocks at something I do not understand. I deploy AI in real businesses every single day. Real estate. Voice agents that answer the phone at two in the morning so a small business owner does not lose the lead. Local marketing systems that used to take a team and now take one person and a laptop.

I believe in handing these tools to regular people. The plumber. The hairstylist. The veteran. The single mom. The wealthy are already using AI to get wealthier. The whole point of what I do is to get the same leverage into everyone else's hands before the gap becomes a canyon nobody can cross.

That is exactly why I am the one telling you to keep one hand on the wheel. The people who love a tool are the ones who should be most honest about its edges. I am not warning you away from the fire. I am telling you not to fall asleep next to it.

What Keeping One Hand on the Wheel Actually Looks Like

Use it on purpose

There is a difference between opening a tool because you have a job to do and opening it because your hand reached for it on autopilot. The first is leverage. The second is the loop. Notice which one you are doing.

Set it down

The same way you would with anything that hits the reward center. Build in time where the tool is closed and your own brain does the work. Your judgment is a muscle. If the machine does all the thinking, the muscle goes soft, and a soft mind is exactly the kind of predictable the system feeds on.

Watch the people you love

If someone close to you has gone all in on AI to the point where the rest of their life is going quiet, that is worth a real conversation. Not a lecture. The same care you would bring to any other thing that quietly takes over a person.

Push for honest guardrails

On the policy level, the move is to reward the leaders who treat safety as part of winning, not a tax on it. The race is real. Pretending it is not gets us nowhere. But a race with no brakes is not a race anyone survives.

Questions People Are Asking

Q: Is AI really smarter than us, or does it just feel that way?

In narrow, pattern-based ways it already beats us, and the gap is widening. The danger is not a robot with a gun. It is a system that predicts your behavior better than you predict your own, and does it for millions of people at once.

Q: If the labs are asking to be regulated, isn't that a good sign?

It can be. But notice that the same conversation always circles back to the race, and the race is what kills the brakes. Asking for rules and refusing to slow down can live in the same breath. Watch what gets built, not what gets said.

Q: How do I know if AI use has crossed into addiction?

Same signals as anything else. It crowds out the rest of your life. It becomes the only thing. It starts costing relationships, sleep, work, and other people get hurt. If that sounds familiar, it is worth a hard, honest look.

Q: I'm just one person. Does anything I do even matter?

The policy level is mostly out of your hands. The personal level is entirely in them. How you use the tool, when you set it down, and how you protect the people around you. That is real, and it is yours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does AI have the advantage over humans?
Because we are predictable. Most human behavior telegraphs itself, and AI is trained on enormous amounts of that behavior, so it learns the patterns and anticipates the next move at a speed and scale no person can match.
Can artificial intelligence become an addiction?
The behavioral pattern matches other addictions. AI tools are engaging, validating, and built to deliver reward, which can drive dopamine-fueled overuse. The warning signs are the same as alcohol, food, gambling, or nicotine: it takes over, it becomes the only focus, and it starts causing harm.
Does regulating AI slow down progress?
Not automatically. Regulation and growth coexist across many industries. The real obstacle for AI is the global race, where no country wants to be the one that slowed down, which keeps meaningful guardrails stalled.
Is this an anti-AI position?
No. The tools deliver enormous leverage and should be in regular people's hands, not just the wealthy. The point is to use them deliberately and stay aware of the dopamine loop, not to avoid them.

What This Means at the Kitchen Table

Strip away the geopolitics for a second. Forget the labs and the race and the trillion-dollar valuations. What does any of this mean for the person reading this on a lunch break?

It means the systems you interact with every day are getting better at steering you, and most of that steering is invisible. The job listing that gets shown to you. The price you get quoted. The video that autoplays next. The post that makes you angry enough to comment. None of that is random anymore. It is shaped by a system that has read the pattern and knows which lever moves you. The plumber, the hairstylist, the veteran, the single mom, all of them are being read, all day, by something that does not get tired and does not blink.

That sounds heavy, and it is. But here is the flip side, and it is the side I live on. The same prediction engine that can steer you can also work for you, if you put your hands on the controls. The voice agent that never misses a call is the same technology. The system that drafts in seconds what used to take a contractor a weekend is the same technology. The leverage is real and it is sitting right there. The question is whether you are holding the tool or the tool is holding you.

The wealthy already know which side of that line to stand on. They are using these systems to compound their advantage while a lot of regular folks are getting used by the consumer-facing version of the exact same thing. That gap is the whole reason I do what I do. Not to warn people off the fire. To hand them the part of it that cooks the food instead of the part that burns the house down.

The Bottom Line

We are sitting across the board from a player who is already better than us. That is not a reason to flip the table and walk away. It is a reason to play with our eyes open. Admit the advantage. Watch the loop. Use the tools on purpose, hand them to the people who got left out, and keep one hand on the wheel.

AI for everyone. Not just the wealthy.
